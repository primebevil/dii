# Paper Seed Notes

A living brain-dump for a possible research paper, captured while the work is still in progress. No structure is owed here and nothing is a conclusion. The goal is to catch the claims and surprises while they are fresh, so that when there are three or four of them the outline already exists. Started Week 4 (2026-07-29).

## The spine, as it currently stands

Monetization is a category error for a sovereignty-motivated distributed system.

The reason to distribute intelligence at all is sovereignty: keeping control out of any single owner's hands. The moment a monetization layer is added on top (a token, a fee, a captured-value mechanism), an owner is reintroduced by the back door, because someone now holds the lever that the value flows through. So the free-with-stewardship model is not a fallback we settled for after failing to find a business model. It is what the sovereignty thesis forces once it is taken seriously. The architecture answered the business-model question for us, and the answer was that there isn't one, there is only stewardship of the infrastructure.

This is the surprising part worth writing down while it is fresh. We went in looking for a way to monetize and came out convinced that monetizing would quietly destroy the thing we were building. Crypto is the cautionary case that makes the argument land: it promised decentralization and then made the token the point, so incentives re-centralized around whoever accumulated the most. The monetization layer eats the thesis.

## Why the two things I named are one paper

Two observations came up in conversation and they turn out to be the same claim from two ends. First, the surprise at landing on stewardship instead of monetization given how everyone else, especially in crypto, tried to monetize. Second, a strong pull toward the "why distribute at all" angle. These are not two topics. "Why distribute" answers with sovereignty, and sovereignty is exactly what rules monetization out. The surprise was the feeling of that logic finishing itself.

## What this reframes

The stewardship choice stops being an admission ("we couldn't figure out monetization") and becomes an argument ("monetization is the wrong category for this kind of system, and here is why"). Contrarian but earned, which is the kind of claim that gets a paper read.

It also relocates the open problem to a more interesting place. If not money, then what sustains the infrastructure? That opens real questions rather than a gap to be embarrassed about:

- Stewardship by whom, and how is that stewardship itself kept from re-centralizing into a de facto owner?
- Funded how, without a funder dependency that could pull the plug? (Opportunistic grants are acceptable; funder capture is not.)
- Governed how?
- Sybil-resistant enough that a free and open door does not simply get kicked in? Identity is the spine of the abuse-resistance story, and it is load-bearing for the whole "free" premise.

These connect to work already in the repo: the proof-of-contribution failure mode in The Case Against DII (Objection 4), the sovereignty survival condition running through Objections 2 and 5, and the governance and abuse-resistance track.

## Candidate framings (not yet chosen)

Three shapes the paper could take, kept here so the choice can be made later from evidence rather than now:

1. The thesis version. Why distribute intelligence at all, with sovereignty as the real motivation and the argument that it is worth the cost. A manifesto with receipts.
2. The hard-problem version. Pick the single gnarliest recurring obstacle (Sybil identity is the current candidate) and treat the paper as "why this is the crux of distributing anything, here is the design space, here is what breaks." Narrower, more defensible, more likely to actually finish.
3. The experience-report version. "We spent N weeks building this, here is what we assumed, here is what turned out to be wrong." Low pretension, high credibility, and it largely writes itself from what already happened.

The monetization-to-stewardship arc is a strong candidate for the opening move in any of the three.
